ABSTRACT
This project proposes the design and implementation of a web-based Blood Donor
Registration and Information Management System for Muslim Hospital, Saki. The system will
provide a structured platform for registering voluntary blood donors, maintaining donor profiles,
recording verified blood group information and donation history, and retrieving donor records
when required. The study is motivated by information-management challenges that can arise
when records are held in paper files or disconnected systems, including slow retrieval, duplicate
entries, outdated contact details, and difficulty preparing summaries. The hospital’s actual
workflow and challenges will first be established through approved requirements gathering.
The project will adopt an iterative system-development approach involving requirements
gathering, analysis, design, implementation, testing, and documentation. Data will be collected
through interviews, observation, and review of approved blank forms, subject to hospital
permission. The application will provide authorised staff with secure access to donor records and
administrative functions. A donor-facing registration feature may also be provided if approved.
The system will support information management only; clinical screening, eligibility decisions,
blood collection, laboratory testing, cross-matching, and transfusion decisions will remain the
responsibility of qualified health professionals. The expected outcome is a usable, secure, and
maintainable application for organising and retrieving donor information.
CHAPTER ONE
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background to the Study
Blood is an essential healthcare resource and may be required during emergencies, childbirth
complications, surgery, severe anaemia, and the treatment of certain medical conditions. Hospitals
need reliable ways to maintain information about potential and previous donors and contact them
when necessary. Organised donor records can help authorised personnel retrieve relevant details,
communicate with registered donors, and review donation history.
Donor information may be maintained in paper registers, spreadsheets, or separate files. As
records increase, searching by blood group, updating contact details, identifying duplicates, and
reviewing donation dates may require manual effort. A web-based system can centralise approved
records in a controlled database and make them accessible to authorised users through a browser.
For Muslim Hospital, Saki, the proposed system will be designed around the hospital’s verified
workflow and will not replace clinical or blood-bank procedures.
1.2 Statement of the Problem
Effective donor information management depends on records being accurate, current,
organised, and retrievable. Where information is stored manually or across separate records, staff
may experience delays locating contacts, difficulty identifying duplicate entries, and challenges
updating details or reviewing donation history. This project addresses the need, if confirmed by
preliminary investigation, for a single web-based platform to support donor registration and
information management at Muslim Hospital, Saki. The current process will be examined before
requirements are finalised.

1.3 Aim and Objectives of the Study
The aim is to design and implement a web-based Blood Donor Registration and Information
Management System for Muslim Hospital, Saki.
The specific objectives are to:
• examine the hospital’s existing process for registering donors and maintaining donor
information;
• identify the functional, security, and operational requirements;
• design a centralised database for approved donor details, blood group information, and donation
records;
• develop a web application for registration, record updating, searching, and authorised
administration;
• implement access control, input validation, and basic data-protection measures; and
• test the system against its functional and usability requirements.
1.4 Research Questions
• How is donor information currently collected, stored, updated, and retrieved at Muslim
Hospital, Saki?
• What functional and security requirements should the proposed system satisfy?
• How can a centralised database improve the organisation and retrieval of donor information?
• To what extent will the developed system meet its functional and usability requirements?
1.5 Significance of the Study
The system is expected to give the hospital an organised means of maintaining donor
information and retrieving records. Authorised staff may spend less time searching and may
identify records matching approved criteria more efficiently. Donors may benefit from a clearer
registration process and an approved means of updating contact details. The project may also
serve as a reference for students and researchers. Benefits will depend on staff adoption, data
quality, infrastructure, and compliance with hospital procedures.
1.6 Scope of the Study
The study will cover donor registration; capture of approved personal and contact
information; recording blood group information as supplied or verified under hospital procedures;
secure staff login; role-based administration; viewing and updating donor profiles; authorised
donation-history entries; basic summaries and reports; testing; and documentation. It is limited to
Muslim Hospital, Saki. The system will not assess medical fitness, diagnose conditions, conduct
laboratory tests, perform blood matching, manage transfusion, or independently declare a person
eligible to donate. Such decisions remain with qualified personnel.
1.7 Limitations of the Study
Possible limitations include restricted access to institutional records, limited time for
interviews and testing, unreliable electricity or network access, and user-adoption challenges.
These will be managed through approved data collection, synthetic test records, user orientation,
and documentation. Real donor information will not be used for demonstrations without
authorisation.
CHAPTER TWO
LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Concept of Blood Donor Information Management
Blood donor information management involves collecting, organising, updating, retrieving,
and protecting information about people who have expressed interest in donating blood or have
previously donated. Administrative records may include a donor’s name, contact details, blood
group where verified, registration date, and donation history. These records support
communication and coordination but do not replace clinical screening or laboratory procedures.
2.2 Manual and Computer-Based Record Management
Manual record systems are familiar and may require little initial technical investment.
However, as records grow, paper-based processes can make searching, updating, backing up, and
reporting more difficult. Computer-based systems can support faster retrieval, consistent data
entry, controlled access, and routine backups. These benefits depend on suitable database design,
staff training, reliable infrastructure, and procedures for correcting inaccurate information.
2.3 Web-Based Information Systems
A web-based information system is accessed through a browser and commonly consists of a
presentation interface, application logic, and a database. It enables authorised users to access
functions from different devices without installing a separate desktop application. A
well-designed system should validate submitted information, protect user accounts, restrict
functions according to roles, and maintain records of important administrative actions.
2.4 Data Security and Privacy in Healthcare Systems
Donor information is personal information and must be handled confidentially. The proposed
system will collect only information required for the approved purpose. Security measures will
include authenticated access, role-based permissions, password hashing, input validation, secure
configuration, database backups, and restricted administrative functions. The project will be
guided by the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and hospital policies. Hospital approval and any
required ethics or privacy review will be obtained before real donor information is collected or
processed.
2.5 Review of Related Systems and Identified Gap
Digital registration and record-management systems demonstrate the usefulness of structured
databases, search functions, and controlled administrative access. A system for a particular
hospital must reflect its workflow, user roles, terminology, and operating conditions. This project
will develop a locally tailored solution for Muslim Hospital, Saki. The specific gap will be
established through requirements gathering and will concern the hospital’s verified need for a
simple, secure platform supporting donor registration and information management.
CHAPTER THREE
SYSTEM ANALYSIS AND METHODOLOGY
3.1 Research and Development Method
The project will use an applied, iterative system-development approach. Requirements will
be gathered and reviewed with intended users, after which the system will be analysed, designed,
implemented, tested, and documented. Feedback obtained during development will be used to
refine the application before final evaluation.
3.2 Methods of Data Collection
Subject to permission, primary information will be obtained through:
• Interviews: discussions with relevant hospital personnel about donor registration, record
retrieval, updating, and reporting.
• Observation: observation of approved workflows to understand task sequences and the
information handled.
• Document review: examination of approved blank registration forms or templates, where
access is granted.
Only information necessary for defining system requirements will be collected. Personal donor
records will not be copied unless the hospital grants explicit approval and safeguards are in place.
3.3 Analysis of the Proposed System
The proposed system will provide donor-facing registration functions, where approved, and
staff-facing management functions. Exact roles and functions will be confirmed during
requirements gathering.
Donor functions: submit a registration form containing approved details; receive confirmation of
submission; and request correction or updating of contact information through the approved
process.
Authorised staff functions: sign in securely; review, verify, approve, update, or deactivate donor
records according to hospital policy; search records using approved fields such as name, blood
group, or location; record authorised donation-related entries; and view or export basic reports.
3.4 Proposed System Architecture and Tools
The system will use a three-layer web architecture. The presentation layer will provide
responsive pages for donors and staff. The application layer will process requests, validate data,
enforce permissions, and apply system rules. The data layer will store donor profiles, user
accounts, and authorised donation-related records. A suitable implementation may use HTML,
CSS, and JavaScript for the interface; Python with Django or FastAPI for the backend; and
PostgreSQL or MySQL for the database. Final technology and hosting choices will be
documented in the project report.

3.5 Functional and Non-Functional Requirements
Functional requirements will include donor registration, staff authentication, role-based
administration, donor-record management, search and filtering, authorised donation-history
recording, and report generation. Non-functional requirements will include usability, reasonable
response time, maintainability, availability, data integrity, privacy, and security. The application
should support modern browsers and adapt to desktop and mobile screens.
3.6 Database Design
The database is expected to contain related tables such as Donors, Users, Roles, Donations,
and AuditLogs. Each donor will have a unique system identifier to reduce duplicate records.
Donors will store approved profile and contact fields; Donations will link authorised donation
entries to a donor; and Users and Roles will control staff access. Audit information will record
important administrative changes where appropriate. The final schema will be refined after
requirements gathering.
3.7 Testing and Evaluation
Unit testing will check individual functions such as form validation and search. Integration
testing will verify communication between the interface, backend, and database. System testing
will examine complete workflows, including registration, staff login, record updates, and report
generation. User acceptance testing will be conducted with selected intended users where the
hospital permits. Synthetic records will be used unless approved access to real data is granted.
Defects identified will be corrected and retested.
3.8 Ethical, Privacy, and Security Considerations
Permission will be sought from the appropriate hospital authority before interviews,
workflow observation, or use of institutional information. Interview participation will be
voluntary. Donor information will be confidential and accessible only to authorised users; donor
contact details will not be publicly searchable. Any public-facing registration feature will submit
information for staff review and will not establish clinical eligibility. Consent wording, data
retention, access rights, and correction or deletion procedures will be agreed with the hospital and
aligned with applicable law and policy.
3.9 Expected Results
The project is expected to produce a working web application, a structured database, user and
technical documentation, and test results. The system should demonstrate that donor information
can be registered, stored, searched, updated, and managed through authorised access. The final
report will evaluate how far the implemented features satisfy the requirements established during
the study.
3.10 Work Plan and Estimated Resources
Work plan: Week 1—requirements gathering; Week 2—system analysis and design; Weeks
3–5—implementation; Week 6—testing and refinement; Week 7—documentation and
submission.
